## Dot orbit

<DocText>A dot per grid cell circles its center at its own pace and breathes with a wave that runs out from the middle; dots near the pointer grow with <code>data-interactive</code>. The grid is measured in CSS pixels, so it stays crisp at any size.</DocText>

```html example label="A breathing grid" schema="none" previewStyle="align-items:stretch;" hint="Move the pointer over it. The controls beside it are generated from the effect: its lists, its parameters and its colors."
<div class="grid gap-4" style="grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fit, minmax(16rem, 1fr))">
  <div
    class="shader"
    id="dot-orbit-demo"
    data-effect="dot-orbit"
    data-interactive
    aria-hidden="true"
    style="--shader-aspect: 16 / 10; border-radius: var(--radius)"
  ></div>
  <div class="shader-controls" data-shader-for="dot-orbit-demo"></div>
</div>
```

  <Variant name="data-orbit" description="How far they circle (0.4)" />
</SkillGrid>

## Trails

<DocText>A line of light follows the pointer and fades, drifting like smoke as it goes; its color runs through the palette. Without a pointer it draws a figure of its own. The trail lives in a texture that each frame reads, fades and draws into again.</DocText>

## Stained glass

<DocText>A Voronoi diagram: every cell is the region closest to one moving point, its glass a color of the palette, lit brighter at its center. The lead between the cells is the true distance to the cell's border - measured along the bisectors with every neighbor - so it keeps one width all around.</DocText>

## Halftone

<DocText>A print screen: the picture is sampled once per cell of a rotated grid and drawn as a dot whose area grows with the darkness - the way a newspaper prints a photo. The grid is measured in CSS pixels. Without a picture it screens a moving pattern of its own.</DocText>

```html example label="A photo as dots" schema="none" hint="The controls beside it are generated from the effect: its lists, its parameters and its colors."

## Matrix

<DocText>Columns of glyphs fall at their own speeds: a bright head, a trail that fades behind it and the glyphs changing as it passes. Each glyph is a 5 x 7 grid drawn in the shader and mirrored so it reads like a character; the grid is measured in CSS pixels, so <code>data-size</code> is the glyph height.</DocText>

## Op art

<DocText>Two sets of rings around two moving centers, combined as an exclusive or - the moire is where they disagree. Every edge is smoothed to the pixel, and where the rings get finer than a pixel they fade to gray instead of breaking into noise. Without <code>data-colors</code> it uses the theme's foreground and background.</DocText>
