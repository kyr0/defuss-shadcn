<h2 id="numeric" style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-size:1.375rem;font-weight:400;letter-spacing:-0.02em;margin:0 0 0.875rem;">Numeric dimensions</h2>

<DocText><code>w-n</code>, <code>h-n</code> and <code>size-n</code> map directly to <code>n × --size-base</code> — <code>w-48</code> is 48 × 4px = 192px at the default root. Width and height are physical axes, matching their CSS names, and density does not change them.</DocText>

<h2 id="fractions" style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-size:1.375rem;font-weight:400;letter-spacing:-0.02em;margin:0 0 0.875rem;">Fractions and full width</h2>

<h2 id="intrinsic" style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-size:1.375rem;font-weight:400;letter-spacing:-0.02em;margin:0 0 0.875rem;">Content-based sizing</h2>

<DocText><code>w-auto</code> defers to the layout algorithm; <code>w-fit</code> shrinks to the content; <code>w-min</code> and <code>w-max</code> use the content's minimum and maximum intrinsic widths. These are the right tools for badges, chips and buttons that should hug their label.</DocText>

<h2 id="constraints" style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-size:1.375rem;font-weight:400;letter-spacing:-0.02em;margin:0 0 0.875rem;">Constraints: min, max and none</h2>

<DocText>Dimensions are constraints, not clipping. <code>max-w-\*</code> caps a box without narrowing the page below the cap; <code>min-h-\*</code> guarantees a floor while content can grow past it; <code>max-w-none</code> removes a cap a component shipped with.</DocText>

<h2 id="logical" style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-size:1.375rem;font-weight:400;letter-spacing:-0.02em;margin:0 0 0.875rem;">Logical dimensions</h2>

<DocText><code>inline-\*</code> and <code>block-\*</code> mirror the numeric scale on the writing-mode axes: inline is width in horizontal writing, height in vertical writing. Below, the same classes — <code>inline-40 block-24 ps-4</code> — in three contexts. The filled area is the content box; the gap the <code>ps-4</code> leaves follows inline-start: left in LTR, right in RTL, top in vertical writing.</DocText>

<h2 id="viewport" style="font-family:var(--font-display);font-size:1.375rem;font-weight:400;letter-spacing:-0.02em;margin:0 0 0.875rem;">Viewport sizes and the app shell</h2>

<DocText><code>h-screen</code> / <code>min-h-screen</code> / <code>max-h-screen</code> are 100vh, with <code>dvh</code>, <code>svh</code> and <code>lvh</code> variants for mobile browser chrome. Their classic use is the app shell — header, fluid content, footer at the bottom even with little content. The demo renders that pattern inside a fixed-height frame; at page level you would write <code>min-h-screen</code> instead of <code>h-full</code>.</DocText>

<Demo viewport>
  <div class="demo-viewport">

        <header class="demo-tile h-10" style="border-radius:0;">header</header>
        <div class="flex-1 min-h-0 demo-tile h-auto" data-variant="muted">flex-1 — content fills whatever is left</div>
        <footer class="demo-tile h-10" data-variant="secondary" style="border-radius:0;">footer — pushed down, never floating mid-page</footer>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <DemoCode header="HTML — at page level">{"<body class=\"min-h-screen stack gap-0\">\n  <header>header</header>\n  <main class=\"flex-1\">content fills whatever is left</main>\n  <footer>footer — pushed down, never floating mid-page</footer>\n</body>"}</DemoCode>
</Demo>

<DocText>Prefer <code>dvh</code> units when content must survive the mobile URL bar collapsing. <code>w-screen</code> is 100vw — it includes the scrollbar and ignores the parent, so <code>w-full</code> is usually the right choice inside a page.</DocText>
